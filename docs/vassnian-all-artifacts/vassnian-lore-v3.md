## 1. THE KINGDOM OF CAELMUND

**Name:** Caelmund  
**People:** Caelmundi  
**Government:** Feudal kingdom ruled by a King  
**History:** Founded after the Sealing War, the ancient conflict that imprisoned the dark god Gorath. The kingdom rose in the aftermath of that victory. Centuries of relative peace have made it strong but complacent.

### The First City (Hub)
The capital and main hub. A walled metropolis of stone and faith.
- **The Citadel** - the King's seat, military command
- **The Market Quarter** - shops, blacksmith, apothecary, taverns
- **The Circle of Arrival** - where Godsents appear (ancient stone summoning platform)
- **The Temple District** - churches to Auron, Maetha, and Luminar. Healers, priests.
- **The Lower Quarter** - common folk, cheaper lodging, information brokers, shadier dealings

### Surface Races

| Race | Status in Caelmund |
|------|--------------------|
| **Humans** | Dominant race, most common, rule the kingdom |
| **Elves** | Forest enclaves, mostly keep to themselves. Ancient, reclusive. |
| **Dwarves** | Mountain fortresses in the overworld. Master forgers and builders. |
| **Orcs** | Former enemies, now grudging allies. Uneasy peace. Some serve in military, many live in border territories. |
| **Halflings/Gnomes** | Merchants, tinkers, innkeepers. Everywhere but unnoticed. Run trade routes and taverns. Valuable as information brokers. |
| **Beastfolk** | Tribal clans, each based on different animals (wolf, cat, bird, lizard, etc.). Each clan has its own culture and territory. |
| **Undead/Cursed** | NOT a race but a condition. Cursed people, feared and shunned by society. Tied to Gorath's corruption. As the seal weakens, more cursed individuals appear. |

**Race relations:** Cities are mixed and cosmopolitan. Rural areas tend to be single-race and more insular. Friction exists but open conflict between races is rare in peacetime.

## 2. THE PANTHEON

### Structure
3 worshipped gods + 1 sealed dark god + 1 hidden/forgotten god.

| God | Domain | Worshipped By | Notes |
|-----|--------|---------------|-------|
| **Auron** | War | Soldiers, knights, paladins, warriors | Noble, protector-warrior. Temples in military districts. |
| **Maetha** | Life | Healers, farmers, common folk, priests | Earth-mother, warm, ancient. Temples in every village. |
| **Luminar** | Knowledge | Mages, scholars, seekers of truth | Classical, cerebral. Great libraries serve as temples. |
| **Gorath** | Conquest, Corruption, Domination | Cultists, the desperate, the power-hungry | SEALED. Not part of accepted pantheon. Considered a demon or fallen god. |
| **Umbrath** | Shadow, Secrets | Forgotten on the surface. Worshipped in the Shadow World. | Hidden/forgotten god. Player discovers shrines in Act 2. Ties into Shadow World lore. |

### Divine Rules
- Gods **never appear directly** to mortals. They communicate through signs, dreams, visions, and their priests/oracles.
- The player never meets any god face-to-face. The divine is felt, not seen.
- **Priest class passive (Divine Favor):** Player picks a deity (Auron, Maetha, or Luminar) at character creation. Unlocks deity-specific story choices throughout the game.

### The Summoning Mystery
A god secretly rigged the Rite of Calling to ensure the player was summoned. **Who did it is deliberately ambiguous.** The player never fully learns the answer. All five gods are suspects:
- Auron (protecting the world through a chosen warrior)
- Maetha (preserving life by selecting the right soul)
- Luminar (calculated the optimal hero)
- Umbrath (working from the shadows for unknown reasons)
- Gorath (perhaps wanting a worthy opponent, or an unwitting pawn)

## 3. GORATH / THE DARK GOD

**Nature:** God of conquest, corruption, and domination. Wants to RULE, not just destroy. Offers power to those who serve him. Persuasive, ancient, patient. He corrupts rather than annihilates.

**Origin:** Waged war on the mortal world thousands of years ago. Commanded armies of demons, corrupted mortals, and twisted creatures. A coalition of mortal heroes plus divine intervention defeated and SEALED him. He could not be destroyed.

**The Seal:** Anchored to sacred sites across the land. After millennia, the binding magic is eroding.

**The Breaking / Progression by Act:**
| Act | Signs of Gorath's Influence |
|-----|-----------------------------|
| 0 | Strange creature behavior, unusual monster sightings, uneasy atmosphere |
| 1 | Monsters growing stronger and organized, dark magic sightings, cultist activity |
| 1.5 | The seal cracks visibly. Betrayal timed to a major weakening. |
| 2 | Player is underground. Surface deteriorates without them. |
| 3 | Gorath's armies emerging, sky darkens, temples losing power, widespread corruption |
| 4 | Gorath nearly free. Final confrontation. |

## 4. THE GODSENT SUMMONING

### The Rite of Calling (Public Knowledge)
An ancient ritual that pulls heroes from other worlds. Requires:
- The Circle of Arrival (ancient stone platform in the First City)
- A coalition of the kingdom's strongest mages and priests
- Immense magical energy (drains kingdom's reserves)
- The King's authorization (desperate measure, not done lightly)

**Why now:** The King's seer foresaw Gorath's avatar approaching. An attack is coming. The King tells the Godsents upfront: your goal is to get stronger and prepare.

The ritual has been used before in Caelmund's history. Godsents are part of legend. Some became great heroes, some failed, a few went dark. The kingdom knows Godsents are powerful but unpredictable.

### Hidden Truth
A god subtly influenced the ritual's targeting to ensure the player was among those summoned. The ritual normally pulls "compatible" souls, essentially random. The god nudged it. The player doesn't learn this until late game, if at all. It reframes the journey: they weren't random. But no god guaranteed success, just gave them a chance.

### Multiple Parties
The current summoning pulled MULTIPLE people from other worlds. Each Godsent was paired with a Caelmundi companion by the King. Multiple parties operate simultaneously. The player isn't the only hero, just the one we follow.

## 5. THE TRAITOR PARTY

### Overview
A party of Godsents who defected to Gorath in secret. They want POWER. They want to rule alongside Gorath in the new world order. Gorath granted them dark power upgrades (stronger skills, forbidden magic, enhanced stats) in exchange for eliminating the other Godsent parties.

### How They Found the Trap
The traitors scouted ahead on their own and discovered an ancient barrier trap in dangerous territory. No divine intel. They were thorough and ruthless. They realized they could use it to eliminate the player.

### The Betrayal (Act 1.5)
1. The King sends the player's party and the traitor party on a joint mission into dangerous territory.
2. The traitors engineer the player to go deeper ("you take point, we'll cover the rear").
3. The player enters willingly. It's a legitimate mission, they trust their allies.
4. An ancient **barrier trap** activates. The player is locked inside.
5. The traitors walk up to the barrier and **mock the player through it**. They reveal everything: the deal with Gorath, that they planned this, that no one escapes.
6. They leave. The surface world is told the player died heroically.
7. The only way out is DEEPER, through the barrier zone into the Shadow World.

## 6. COMPANION SYSTEM

### Rules
- **Max party size:** Player + up to 3 companions
- **Total companions in game:** 13-16
- **Races:** Any race (humans, elves, dwarves, orcs, halflings, beastfolk, etc.)
- **Death rule:** Any party member (player OR companion) who takes 3rd injury = DEAD. **Any death = GAME OVER.** Entire run ends.

### Recruitment Path

**First Companion:**
- The King offers 3 random companions from his "guild" after the player arrives.
- Player picks 1, or can REFUSE ALL (run solo).
- The first companion asks to have a conversation with you. This conversation serves triple duty:
  1. **Quest start** - accepting begins their personal quest line. Refusing fails it.
  2. **Lore delivery** - the companion asks what you want to know. Player picks topics to learn about the world.
  3. **Relationship** - establishes the companion as a person, not just a combat tool.
- "Enough for now" is always available to skip or end the conversation early.

**Later Companions:**
- Found through recruitment quests that appear randomly during missions.
- Each companion has a unique "joining quest," a short mission featuring them.
- After the joining quest, player chooses to ACCEPT or DENY.
- Recruitment quests keep appearing until the player has 3 companions.
- Player can deny everyone and run solo if they want.

### Companion Personal Quests
- Every companion has a personal side quest chain.
- **Success:** Companion becomes stronger (stat boost, new skill, lore unlock).
- **Failure:** Companion stays the same (no punishment, just missed upgrade).
- **Companion lines:** Every companion quest includes companion_lines for other party members. Companions react to each other's stories, showing personality even during someone else's quest.

### Companion Concepts (Examples from Design)
- **The Sly Rogue** - you help them commit a crime. If you go along with it, they respect you and offer to join.
- **The Fallen Hero** - a one-armed former champion. Recruitment quest introduces them. Personal quest chain: forge an iron arm at the dwarven mountain fortress, restoring their strength.
- **The Bound Mage** - a mage who made a secret deal with a demon. Personal quest: help her break the contract. Success makes her very powerful; failure keeps her limited.

## 7. OTHER GODSENT PARTIES

Multiple Godsent parties exist beyond the player and the traitors. They add life to the world.

### The Meeting of Heroes (Act 0)
At the start of the game, all summoned Godsent groups meet to talk and plan. They discuss where they came from and how to act. Present at this meeting:
- The traitor hero (seems friendly, charismatic)
- The Shadow Hero (a rogue-type, unimpressive at first)
- Other Godsent groups the player can encounter later in the story

### The Shadow Hero
A mysterious rogue-type Godsent. Unimpressive and easy to overlook at the initial meeting. A mystery figure throughout the entire game. Can be encountered again in Act 2 (Shadow World). May appear in the final act to weaken the final boss with a sneak attack. We will design this character carefully.

### Joint Missions
- Happen across **all acts**, missions where you fight alongside another party.
- **Almost never in Act 2** (player is trapped in Shadow World).
- In Act 3, returning parties may have changed (some dead, some stronger, some gone dark).

### Shadow World Godsent Encounters
Two special encounters in Act 2:
1. **Dead Party:** The player finds the remains of a Godsent party that ventured into the Shadow World and died. A dark, grounding moment. "This could be us."
2. **Secret Living Party:** A Godsent party that went to the Shadow World **on purpose**, seeking forbidden knowledge or power. They're alive but changed. Moral gray area: scholars, power-seekers, or have they gone too far? Potential allies or enemies depending on player choices.

## 8. THE SHADOW WORLD

### What It Is
An ancient underground realm, far older than Caelmund. **NOT Gorath's domain.** Completely independent. Has its own sky (bioluminescent cavern ceilings, glowing crystals like dim stars), ecosystems, civilizations, and politics.

### Who Lives There

**Deepkin** - unique races not found on the surface, adapted to the underground over millennia:
- **Fungal Deepkin** - beings connected to vast underground fungal networks. May communicate through spores. Alien but not hostile by nature.
- **Crystal Deepkin** - beings made of or integrated with living crystal. Found near crystal formations. Ancient, slow, possibly very wise.
- **Shadow Deepkin** - shadow-adapted humanoids. Pale, dark-eyed, move silently. Most common Deepkin type. Have their own cities and culture.

**Dark Mages & Exiles** - surface dwellers who fled underground across generations. Persecution, forbidden magic, hiding from enemies. Form their own communities in underground cities.

**Ancient Dragons** - lair in the deepest caverns. Not all hostile. Some territorial, some sleeping, some scholars who hoard knowledge. Remnants of a previous age.

**Umbrath Worshippers** - the hidden god Umbrath has shrines and followers in the Shadow World. Forgotten on the surface, still active below. The player discovers this faith during Act 2.

**Monsters** - shadow creatures, crystal beasts, cave horrors, ancient constructs left by forgotten builders.

### Underground Cities (at least 2-3)
- A **dark mage enclave** - merchants, information, forbidden knowledge
- An **ancient Deepkin city** - alien culture, unique quests, potential allies
- A **dangerous frontier settlement** - monsters at the gates, desperate people

### Tone of Act 2
Survival. Isolation. Discovery. The player has been betrayed, cast down, separated from the surface. They must:
1. Survive the immediate aftermath (wounded, lost, alone except for companion)
2. Find civilization (underground cities)
3. Navigate Shadow World politics (nobody trusts surface-dwellers)
4. Discover ancient lore (possibly about Gorath's original sealing, or Umbrath)
5. Find a way back to the surface

|-----|----------|
| 0-1 | Curiosity, cautious respect. "Another Godsent? The King must be desperate." |
| 2 | Shadow World NPCs are wary: "Surface-dweller? You won't last." |

### Mission Selection
Players choose from **3 mission options** unless it's a required/main story mission. This means harder missions (like a dragon fight in Act 3) exist in the pool but players opt in. Most players won't pick the hardest option unless their party is strong or they're feeling brave.

### Mission Types
- **Required:** Story-critical, must complete to advance acts
- **Random:** Side missions with random combat/rewards, pulled from a pool per act
- **Companion Quests:** Recruitment quests + personal quests
- **Continuation Stories:** Multi-act stories spanning 2+ acts (tags track state)
- **One-Shots:** Self-contained single missions, no sequel
- **Joint Missions:** Alongside other Godsent parties

### Act 1 Enemies
Practical overworld threats, not Gorath's forces directly. The chaos is growing as the seal weakens, but the enemies are grounded: orc chieftains, goblin chieftains, bandit groups, monster packs, village threats.

### Act 2 Bosses
Shadow World threats: dark mages, small dragonlike beasts (not full-grown dragons), strong undead warriors, Deepkin hostiles, ancient constructs.

### Act 3 Bosses
Varied. A full dragon is available but rare, mega strong, and most players won't pick that mission. Other bosses tied to the espionage/shadow war storyline.

## 11. KEY LORE FACTS (Quick Reference)

1. Caelmund was founded after the Sealing War. The kingdom rose from that victory, not built on the prison itself.
2. The seal is weakening. Signs escalate through the acts.
3. Gorath wants to rule, not just destroy. He corrupts and offers power.
4. The King's seer foresaw Gorath's avatar approaching. This triggered the summoning.
5. The summoning was secretly rigged by an unknown god. The player was specifically chosen. This is never definitively answered.
6. All Godsent groups meet at the start (Act 0) to talk and plan.
7. 4 traitor party presets with unique leaders rotate per playthrough.
8. The traitors defected for power and found the barrier trap themselves.
9. The Shadow Hero is a mysterious rogue Godsent, a thread running through the entire game.
10. The Shadow World is independent of Gorath. Ancient, with its own civilizations.
11. Umbrath, the forgotten god of Shadow/Secrets, is worshipped only in the Shadow World.
12. Deepkin (fungal, crystal, shadow subtypes) are unique Shadow World races.
13. 13-16 companions exist. Player gets first from the King's guild (pick 1 of 3), rest from recruitment quests.
14. First companion conversation = quest start + lore delivery + relationship building.
15. Max 3 companions. Any death (player or companion, 3rd injury) = game over.
16. Elves are reclusive (forest enclaves). Orcs are former enemies, uneasy allies. Halflings are everywhere but unnoticed. Beastfolk are tribal clans.
17. Undead/Cursed is a condition, not a race. Tied to Gorath's corruption.
18. Gods never appear directly. Communication through signs, dreams, priests only.
19. Act 3 is a shadow war. Player returns in secret, operates covertly. Not loud revenge.
20. Players choose from 3 missions unless it's a required story mission.
